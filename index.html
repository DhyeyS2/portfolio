I'm Dhyey. I design, build and fly rockets, and I learn mostly by getting things wrong first.

It started in a field in Surat, watching a model rocket leave the ground and disappear in seconds. Years later I watched Falcon 9 land for the first time and wanted to understand every part of how a system that big could fly and come back down. Since then I have built solid rocket motors, flight computers, aircraft and a CubeSat prototype, first in India and now in the UK.

Almost everything I have built failed before it worked. My first rocket never left the pad when its motor failed, which led to three failed static fires, a redesign from first principles, and finally a CNC-machined KNSB motor producing over 200 N. My first RC plane never flew at 13. I rebuilt it at 16 using the lift equation and thrust-to-weight sizing from HL Physics and Maths, and it flew. The difference was not effort, it was understanding.

Right now I am building a finless rocket stabilised by thrust vector control, tuning its PID controller in a Simulink model before flight, and contributing to a research project comparing five propellant formulations through static-fire testing.

What I enjoy most is the full loop: turning maths and physics into hardware, testing it for real, and using the data to make the next version better. Long term, I want to build my own company in rocketry and space technology.
